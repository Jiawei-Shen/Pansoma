**Comment（要点）。**

- HapMap 的 positives 是 germline variant，它们的 donor 在 HPRC 图里，所以理论上 pangenome 比对会把它们去掉。

**Response.**

We included the SMaHT HAPMAP6 mixture to explore how Pansoma performs at low allele fractions: it is deep (about 500x) and has a large truth set at allele fractions of 0.25–16.5%, well below what our tumor dilutions reach. We agree that it has three limits for a pangenome-based tumor-only caller:

1. Its positives are germline alleles of the five minor donors that the HG005 background lacks. They are not acquired mutations, and mixing donors does not reproduce tumor mutational processes, clonal evolution or copy-number changes.
2. Four of the donors are in the HPRC v1.1 graph (d9 filter), and all six are in the HPRC v2.1 graph (d46 filter); the submitted HapMap results used a graph built without any of them (R1.5). Positives common among the graph's other individuals are still paths of that graph: reads carrying them align without an edit, and only about 1% of these SNV positives on chr1 have a tensor. The trained classifier uses this population prior through its haplotype-count channel (R1.1).
3. The donors are public reference samples. Their rare alleles are found in CoLoRSdb, one of our population filtering resources, far more often than those of our cancer benchmarks' normals (chr20 SNVs: 14.5–25.9% against 4.0–7.9%; R2.6), so filtering also works against these positives: it tags 96.3% of the mixture's SNV positives and 40.4% of its indel positives on chr20.

The mixture therefore measures sensitivity to low-fraction germline alleles. It does not measure tumor-only somatic calling.

We removed it from the main benchmark. The Supplement reports it only on a graph without any of the six donors (40 HPRC individuals plus GRCh38 and CHM13), as the empirical sensitivity by allele fraction in this mixture, before population filtering and for alleles that are not graph paths (R2.4), not as somatic accuracy. For tumor-only calling we use tumor/normal dilutions that contain true somatic mutations: COLO829T at 75, 50 and 25% purity on three platforms, with one model per platform trained on chr2–22 of all purities and tested per purity on chr1; the tumor itself is not held out, so these results describe a held-out chromosome of one tumor, not other tumors (R1.5, R2.4). Differences in base quality between tumor- and normal-derived reads may make the Illumina and ONT dilution results optimistic; R1.5 describes this and reports both after removing it.
