**Comment（要点）。**

- HapMap 的 "somatic-like" positives 是少数个体（minor donors）的 germline allele；Pansoma 的前提是图会压掉 population germline allele，两者冲突，这个 benchmark 可能系统性地对 pangenome caller 不利，也不是获得性低 VAF 突变的合适检验。

**Response.**

We agree with the first point. On the second, the submitted HapMap results used a graph built without any of the six mixture donors, so the donors' presence in the standard HPRC graphs did not apply to this analysis. That graph had the same Minigraph-Cactus construction and d9 filter but was built from 40 HPRC year-1 individuals plus GRCh38 and CHM13 (82 haplotypes; HG00438, HG02257, HG02486, HG02622, HG002, HG005 and NA19240 excluded); the Methods now state this. Four of the donors are in the HPRC v1.1 graph and all six in the HPRC v2.1 graph (d46 filter), as our companion study noted, which is why the donor-free graph was used. The HAPMAP6 positives are germline alleles of the five minor donors (evaluated at VAF 1–16.5%), not acquired mutations. The donors' rare alleles are also found in CoLoRSdb, one of our population filtering resources, far more often than those of our cancer benchmarks' normals (chr20 SNVs: 14.5–25.9% against 4.0–7.9%). The benchmark therefore asks the caller to report alleles that its population prior, used by the trained model (R2.1), is designed to suppress.

We removed the HapMap mixture from the main benchmark and withdrew the HapMap-trained low-VAF model as a somatic model. We keep the mixture only to measure empirical sensitivity by allele fraction, rerun with the current pipeline on the donor-free graph and scored without population filtering, which does not apply to its germline positives (Supplement; R2.4); no claim about somatic accuracy rests on it. Low-VAF performance is now measured on in-silico dilutions of COLO829T with COLO829BL on three platforms (nominal purity 75, 50 and 25%; measured 0.66 / 0.40 / 0.20 for Illumina and Fiber-seq, 0.70 / 0.44 / 0.22 for ONT). One model per platform and variant type is trained on chr2–22 of all four purities and tested on chr1 of each; the tumor itself is not held out. Results by purity and allele fraction are in R2.4 (Fiber-seq SNV F1 after population filtering 0.665–0.779 across purities). Two limitations apply:

- At 75 and 50% purity the false positives have a germline-like spectrum (cosine to the germline spectrum 0.788 and 0.768, against 0.536 at full purity), consistent with a contribution from the tumor's loss of heterozygosity: where the tumor lost the ALT copy of a germline heterozygous SNV, the admixed normal reads put that allele at (1 − purity)/2, inside the somatic allele-fraction range of those mixtures.
- In the Illumina and ONT mixtures, tumor- and normal-derived reads differ in base quality, a cue that a real tumor lacks, so their scores can be optimistic. With per-read base-quality centring, the Illumina dilutions' INDEL best F1 (upper bound) falls to 0.133–0.146, below the 100% sample (0.182), and their SNV PASS F1 from 0.804–0.840 to 0.678–0.709. These runs also changed the quality normalization and, for SNVs, the batch size, so not all of the decrease can be attributed to the cue. For ONT, two models that differ only in this centring (same code, hardware, seed, recipe and normalization) give SNV PASS F1 0.733 / 0.721 / 0.691 / 0.714 without centring and 0.724 / 0.693 / 0.666 / 0.700 with it (100 / 75 / 50 / 25% purity; chr1, after population filtering). In neither does a dilution score above the undiluted sample, and centring costs 0.009 at 100% purity and 0.014–0.028 in the dilutions, so the cue adds at most about 0.02 to the ONT dilution scores.

These low-purity results come from one tumor.
